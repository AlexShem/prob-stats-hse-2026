Seminar 3: discrete random variables, expected value, variance and the binomial

PDF · Solutions appear here after the seminar.

\[ \newcommand{\E}{\mathbb{E}} \newcommand{\Var}{\operatorname{Var}} \newcommand{\Cov}{\operatorname{Cov}} \newcommand{\Corr}{\operatorname{Corr}} \newcommand{\Prob}{\mathbb{P}} \newcommand{\R}{\mathbb{R}} \]

Material from Lecture 3 (3  Discrete random variables). The firms and people on this sheet are made up, and so are their numbers. In Problems 1 to 6 the sheet tells you what \(X\) is and what its distribution is. In Problem 7 you write \(X\), \(n\) and \(p\) down yourself, and Problem 8 asks whether a story is binomial at all. The starred Problem 9* is harder: it is for a strong group or to take home. As before, work by hand first and let the Python check you.

Problem 1

Short drill.

Pavel sells bicycle spare parts on an online marketplace and ships every order from his garage in Tula. Let \(X\) be the delivery time of one parcel, in days. From last season’s records, \(X\) takes the values 2, 5 and 7 with probabilities 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2. (Seven days is Sakhalin and the Far East. Pavel has stopped apologising for it.)

\(x\) 2 5 7
\(p(x)=\Prob(X=x)\) 0.5 0.3 0.2
  1. The values of \(X\) in a list are \(x_1, x_2, x_3\). Write down \(x_1\), \(x_2\), \(x_3\) and \(p(x_1)\), \(p(x_2)\), \(p(x_3)\). Then write \(\sum_{i=1}^{3}p(x_i)\) out term by term and find it.
  2. Find \(F(4)\) and \(F(7)\). Say in words what \(F(4)\) means for Pavel’s customers.
  3. Find \(\E[X]\). Write the sum out before you add.
  4. Find \(\Var(X)\) with the shortcut formula, and \(\operatorname{SD}(X)\). Give the units.

Problem 2

Main problem.

Madina makes soap by hand in Makhachkala and sells it through her own online shop. Lavender soap sells out every December; the rest of the year is calmer. Let \(X\) be the number of orders she gets on one day. From last year’s records,

\(x\) 0 1 2 3 4 5
\(p(x)=\Prob(X=x)\) 0.23 0.20 0.18 0.17 0.13 0.09

and she never gets more than 5 orders a day.

  1. Check that this table is a valid PMF. What two things do you check?
  2. Make a table of the CDF, \(F(x)\) for \(x=0,1,\dots,5\). Then find \(F(2.5)\) and say what it means.
  3. Find the probability that Madina gets more than 1 but at most 4 orders. Write the event with \(X\), list the values it contains, and use the CDF. Then find the probability of 1 to 4 orders, both included.
  4. Find \(\E[X]\).
  5. Find \(\E[X^2]\), \(\Var(X)\) and \(\operatorname{SD}(X)\).

Problem 3

Changing units.

Madina’s shop again. Given: \(X\) = the number of orders on one day, with

\(x\) 0 1 2 3 4 5
\(p(x)\) 0.23 0.20 0.18 0.17 0.13 0.09

\(\E[X]=2.04\), \(\Var(X)=2.6184\) and \(\operatorname{SD}(X)\approx1.618\) (Problem 2). Each order leaves Madina 450 rubles after materials and postage. The platform that hosts her shop charges 600 rubles a day, orders or no orders. Her daily profit in rubles is \[ Y=450X-600 . \]

  1. What is \(Y\) on a day with no orders? With 5 orders?
  2. Find \(\E[Y]\), \(\Var(Y)\) and \(\operatorname{SD}(Y)\).
  3. Where did the 600 rubles go in \(\Var(Y)\)? Explain in one or two sentences.
  4. On what share of days does Madina lose money?

Problem 4

Find the mistake.

The problem was:

The number of customers \(W\) of a food truck on one day has \(\E[W]=50\) and \(\Var(W)=16\). Each customer pays 300 rubles, and the spot costs 4,000 rubles a day, so the daily profit is \(300W-4000\) rubles. Find the expected value and the standard deviation of the daily profit.

Dmitri wrote this solution.

\(\E[300W-4000]=300\cdot50-4000=11{,}000\) rubles.

\(\Var(300W-4000)=300^2\cdot\Var(W)-4000^2=90{,}000\cdot16-16{,}000{,}000=1{,}440{,}000-16{,}000{,}000=-14{,}560{,}000\).

You can’t take the square root of a negative number, so I drop the minus: \(\operatorname{SD}=\sqrt{14{,}560{,}000}\approx3{,}816\) rubles.

  1. Which line is right? Find the mistakes in the other two.
  2. Why can a variance never be negative? Answer from the definition, in one sentence.
  3. Give the correct variance and standard deviation.

Problem 5

Short drill.

Ravil manages a pharmacy in Cheboksary. Since October the cashiers offer every customer a free loyalty card at the till. One customer accepts it with probability 0.9. Let \(X=1\) if the customer accepts and \(X=0\) if not. So \(X\) has the Bernoulli distribution with \(p=0.9\).

  1. Write the PMF of \(X\) as a table.
  2. Find \(\E[X]\), \(\E[X^2]\), \(\Var(X)\) and \(\operatorname{SD}(X)\) from the definitions. Check \(\Var(X)\) with the formula \(p(1-p)\).
  3. What does \(\E[X]=0.9\) mean for 1,000 customers?

Problem 6

Main problem.

Ravil’s pharmacy again. Given: each customer accepts the free loyalty card with probability 0.9, independently of the others. On Monday morning 10 customers come to the till. Let \(X\) be the number of them who accept the card. Then \(X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(10,\,0.9)\).

  1. Ravil’s head office wants 80% of customers to accept. Find the probability that exactly 80% of the 10 accept.
  2. Find the probability that at least 9 of the 10 accept.
  3. Find the probability that at most 8 accept. Use (b).
  4. Find \(\E[X]\), \(\Var(X)\) and \(\operatorname{SD}(X)\).

Problem 7

Name \(X\) yourself.

Alina does e-mail marketing for an online electronics shop in Tomsk. On Tuesday, 100 customers left items in their basket without paying, and the shop sends each of them one reminder e-mail. The e-mails go out at 9 pm, when, Alina says, people feel guilty about their baskets. Each reminder leads to a purchase with probability 0.25, independently of the others.

  1. Write “\(X\) = …” in words. Check the four conditions of the binomial distribution one by one, then write \(n=\dots\), \(p=\dots\) and \(X\sim\dots\).
  2. Find \(\E[X]\), \(\Var(X)\) and \(\operatorname{SD}(X)\). What do they say to Alina?
  3. Find the probability that none of the 100 reminders leads to a purchase. If that happened one Tuesday, what would you tell Alina?

Problem 8

Binomial or not?

For each story, either write \(X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)\) with the numbers, or say which condition of the binomial distribution fails, in one line.

  1. A wholesale bakery supplier in Ivanovo sends out 20 invoices this month. Each invoice is paid late with probability 0.1, independently of the others. \(X\) = the number of invoices paid late.
  2. At an office party, each player is dealt 5 cards from one shuffled pack of 52 cards, 13 of which are hearts. \(X\) = the number of hearts a player gets.
  3. A flower shop has 8 regular corporate customers. The chance that a customer orders next week is 0.2 for a small dental clinic, 0.9 for a hotel, and something in between for the other six. \(X\) = the number of the 8 who order next week.
  4. \(X\) = the number of visitors to Madina’s soap shop website between 8 and 9 pm tonight.
  5. A team of 12 sales consultants in a furniture store in Voronezh can get a quarterly bonus. The team lead decides once, for the whole team: either all 12 get it or nobody does, with probability 0.5 each way. \(X\) = the number of consultants who get a bonus.

Problem 9*

If time allows.

Nargiz is a sales manager at a firm in Irkutsk that installs security cameras in shops. This month she has 4 negotiations, and each ends with a signed contract with probability 0.3, independently of the others. Let \(X\) be the number of contracts she signs, so \(X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(4,\,0.3)\). Her bonus is 5,000 rubles per signed contract, plus an extra 10,000 rubles if she signs all 4.

  1. Make the PMF table of \(X\).
  2. Write down the bonus for each value of \(X\). Why can we not use \(\E[aX+b]=a\E[X]+b\) here?
  3. Find the expected bonus.
  4. A colleague says: “She expects \(\E[X]=1.2\) contracts, so her expected bonus is \(5000\cdot1.2=6000\) rubles.” What did the colleague leave out? How much does the extra 10,000 rubles cost the firm per month, on average?
