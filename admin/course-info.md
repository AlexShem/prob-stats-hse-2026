## Grading

Official formula from the programme (ПУД), module 3 2026/27 interim assessment, which is also the final grade. HSE 10-point scale. Public: the book front page and L00 show the weights, how each part is graded and the retake rule.

| Element | Weight | Module | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quiz in Probability Theory | 0.05 | 2 | 4 quizzes, online, no proctoring |
| Seminar activity (module 2) | 0.05 | 2 | 6 controls |
| Mid-term exam in Probability Theory | 0.25 | 2 | written, offline |
| Quiz in Mathematical Statistics | 0.05 | 3 | 4 quizzes, online, no proctoring |
| Seminar activity (module 3) | 0.05 | 3 | 6 controls |
| Attendance | 0.10 | 2–3, graded in 3 | recorded at the start of each seminar |
| Final exam | 0.45 | 3, exam session | written, offline; blocking element ("блокирует все") |

Weights sum to 1.

- **Quizzes.** Each 10–15 minutes from the start of an attempt, on the previous topics. Multiple choice, short answer, or a photo of a handwritten solution. Each quiz is out of 10. Automatic marking for MC/short answer; uploaded solutions are marked for correctness, consistency and structure. The quiz grade is a weighted sum (weights may differ by difficulty). Drafted privately in `assessment/quizzes/`, uploaded to the LMS by Aleksandr.
- **Seminar activity.** Counts problems a student presents at seminars. The official wording also mentions regular homework; this course has **no homework**, so activity = board presentations only.
- **Attendance.** Share of seminars attended × 10 (e.g. 9 of 14 → 6.43).
- **Midterm, final exam.** Each task has points; the total is converted to 0–10. The final exam score is not rounded.
- **Retake.** If the total is below 3.5. The retake covers the whole course (probability and statistics).
- Rounding of the final grade: TODO (HSE default unless Aleksandr says otherwise).

Example tasks listed in the programme (for reference, not for reuse as-is in the book):

- Probability quiz: $P(A)=0.2$, $P(B)=0.4$, independent; $P(A\mid B)$ = ? (a) 0.5 (b) 0.2 (c) 0.08 (d) none.
- Statistics quiz: $n=10$, $\sum x=0$, $\sum x^2=60$, $\sum y=24$, $\sum y^2=234$, $\sum xy=-87$; find the sample correlation.
- Seminar activity: helpline call time, past mean 16.0 min; after training, $n=20$, $\bar x=14.3$, $s=5.0$; test whether training worked.
- Final exam: the programme attaches "Exam example.pdf" (not in the repo).

## Learning outcomes

Official list from the programme (2019 wording, kept verbatim). Admin only, not shown in the book.

1. Be aware of different definitions of probability, the axioms of probability and their use for derivation of major probabilistic relationships. Know the basic counting methods and principles of combinatorics.
2. Be able to use the concept of conditional probability, law of total probability, notion of independence, collectively exhaustive events.
3. Understand what is meant by a random variable. Know how to use the probability mass functions for calculating the basic characteristics of a discrete random variable (expected value, variance). Be aware of commonly used discrete distributions.
4. Know and be able to use alternative ways of describing a continuous random variable - probability density function and cumulative distribution function. Know how to calculate basic characteristics of a continuous random variable. Be aware of commonly used continuous distributions.
5. Know how to work with a multivariate random variable using the joint probability distribution. Be able to detect the indepedent random variables, calculate the marginal and conditional distributions, covariance and correlation between the variables.
6. Be able to use the methods of descriptive statistics to summarize and visualize the raw data.
7. Be able to derive and use the main sampling distributions - for mean, proportion and variance
8. Be able to estimate the main characteristics of a random variable by means of point or interval estimates. Know how to construct and interpret the confidence intervals for mean, proportion and variance.
9. Understand the principles of hypothesis testing. Be able to perform tests for population mean, proportion and variance.
10. Be able to build, assess the quality and make predictions with the linear regression model.

(The programme's separate 2021 "goals" list is deliberately not recorded here.)

## Annotation (module 3, official)

This is second half of the course Probability Theory and Mathematical Statistics which is a core mathematical subject and is dedicated to statistical part of the course. The statistical section starts with the descriptive techniques but quickly switches to the inferential methods. The topics covered here include sampling distributions, point and interval estimates, hypothesis testing. We conclude with a univariate and, if time permits, multivariate regression. This course is the basis for the development of students' skills in probabilistic and statistical thinking, needed for analysis and modeling in management.
